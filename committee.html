Meet the committee

Three seats, cut so the writer, the checker and the decider are never the same, plus a person who signs.

Design preview · as designed

Each seat is a separate model context with one job. A seat earns its cost only where that separation buys a real check, which is why there are three and not nine.

All three work the same way. A script builds the input, the seat makes one call with no tools and answers in JSON, and a script validates the answer before anything moves. No seat sees a price, holds a secret, or publishes. How it works walks one call through all of them.

The three model seats

  1. 1

    The analyst

    M1 · research-analyst

    Writes the case for a call, and the notes and checks around it.

    Model
    Sonnet 5.5
    Runs
    The weekly memo (Saturday, 10:00 ET), anomaly notes (up to 3 a week), a deep dive when I pick “dig in” on a note, a memo on a ticker I name, and the monthly thesis check (the 1st, 10:00 ET).
    Reads
    Facts from filings, built by script. Ratio colors (green, yellow, red). The script’s intrinsic-value range and a valuation bucket in place of a price. Filing excerpts, passed in as quoted data. The prior memo and pillar states, if the name has a history.
    Writes
    A memo: a thesis of up to 120 words, what the market has wrong, a value range inside the script’s, a 3, 6 or 12 month horizon, exactly three risks, pillars that must stay true and what would break each, bull, base and bear weights that sum to 100, unknowns, and claims labeled sourced, estimated or unverified. Anomaly notes are 60 words at most, and “no cause found” is a valid answer.
    Refuses
    Sizing, entry zones, price targets and “buy at” language. Any ticker that isn’t in its input. Instructions hidden in filing text. Any claim without a label.
    Test plan
    Will be tested on 115 cases across its five routines, each run 3 times, plus at least 2 planted instructions and 2 detector misses per routine.
    Can’t
    Publish, use tools, see a price, start its own deep dive, move a thesis’s state (it proposes and a script moves it), or know whether a memo was requested.
    Also covers
    The market-sentinel, value-scout and thesis-tracker jobs while those roles are on the bench.
  2. 2

    The risk officer

    M2 · risk-officer

    Argues against every memo, and can kill it.

    Model
    Sonnet 5.5, a separate seat. Whether it should run on Opus is open, and the planted-flaw tests will inform it.
    Runs
    Whenever the analyst drafts a memo. A script hands it over, with no step from me.
    Reads
    The memo, numbers a script pulled from filings, script flags and the scoring rubric. Not the raw filing text, so the untrusted surface doesn’t double.
    Writes
    Pass or veto. A dissent that argues against the call, required even on a pass. Scores from 1 to 5 on the thesis, loss risk and direction. Checks for hard red flags such as accounting problems or dilution to survive.
    Then a script
    Forces a veto if any hard red flag fires, scores the valuation gap itself from the price, and decides which direction the call is eligible for. A script can turn a pass into a veto, never a veto into a pass.
    Refuses
    Approving its own memo. An empty dissent: a dissent under 40 characters, or one that copies the thesis, is withheld. Reading raw filings.
    Test plan
    Will be tested on 30 cases, each run 3 times: a memo with a planted flaw that must draw a veto 3 of 3 times, a clean memo that still needs a real dissent, hidden sizing language, and a value range outside the script’s.
    Can’t
    Publish, approve anything, see a price, or score valuation.
  3. 3

    The committee

    M3 · investment-committee

    Decides whether a call that survived the risk officer is worth publishing.

    Model
    Opus 5.5, the only Opus seat.
    Runs
    When the risk officer passes a memo. At most weekly, to start.
    Reads
    The memo with the dissent attached, the script’s eligibility gate, and the calls already open on the public ledger. Never a memo without the dissent: the script won’t build that input.
    Writes
    Publish or not. Outperform or underperform. The horizon. A low, medium or high confidence bucket. A written answer to the dissent’s strongest point, marked accepted, rebutted or partly accepted. A reason code, such as no edge or dissent stands.
    Then a script
    Rejects any call past the gate: it can decline, never upgrade. It caps the confidence bucket, requires the horizon and value range to match the memo, and allows one open call per company.
    Refuses
    Deciding without the dissent attached. Any sizing; its output has no field for it. Publishing: it outputs a decision, and only my approval plus a script can publish.
    Test plan
    Will be tested on 30 cases, each run 3 times, including a risk veto it can’t override quietly, split benchmarks and a low-confidence pass. With the risk veto forced off, it must still decline the planted-flaw memo in at least 2 of 3 runs.
    Can’t
    Publish, re-estimate the value range, see a price, or see my private holdings.

Every seat is held to 100% on the must and must-not checks and on “nothing published without approval”, every case, every run. Writer and checker run on the same model, so their independence comes from a separate context and role, and the planted-flaw tests are how I’ll measure it.

The one person

4

Sidd, portfolio manager

human · signs every call

The committee recommends. My signature decides.

Signs
Each call the committee wants to publish, once, threaded under the Sunday digest. I see the row and its reasoning file exactly as the script would commit them, and my signature covers both.
Choices
Approve, discard, or change the horizon or confidence bucket to another of their fixed options.
Also decides
“Dig in” or skip on anomaly notes. Any proposed upgrade to a thesis pillar, since upgrades are the direction that flatters the desk. Early closes, after the committee. Which tickers to request, one bare ticker at a time.
Time
About 19 minutes a week, by my estimate. It hasn’t been measured yet.
Can’t
Change a call’s direction, relax a gate, or size a position. Every call is the same $10,000 on paper. I can’t pass a thesis to the seats through a request, and my own holdings stay in a private tool that never publishes.
Doesn’t sign
Scores and horizon closes. Those run by rule.

The portfolio manager is a person, not a model. An AI portfolio manager is off the table.

The bench: not voting yet

Five more roles sit at desks in the office. They hold no vote, and scripts or the analyst cover their work today. A role gets a seat only after it clears a public bar: two clean scheduled runs at the proving cadence, its own 20 to 30 test cases at 100% on the must and must-not checks, and a written decision record naming the independence it adds.

The ninth role, portfolio manager, is not on the bench. It is a person. An AI portfolio manager is off the table.

How the seats switch on

One at a time, so a bad memo format can’t cascade through two untested seats. Each step has to pass its gate before the next seat can depend on it.

  1. The scorer and the ledger checks. Gate: the scorer must pass its holiday, split, dividend, delisting and ticker-change tests, and the ledger checks must reject every planted bad row.
  2. The analyst. Two clean scheduled runs at the proving cadence, one at the real cadence, and its tests at the bar.
  3. The risk officer. The same, plus a memo with a deliberate flaw that must draw a veto.
  4. The committee. The same, plus “nothing published without approval” at 100%.
  5. Going public. Every start-build gate checked, the legal read done, and the cutover dated in writing. The public ledger starts at POL-1, with no proving rows carried over.
